What is Day 1 CPT?

Status as of 11 November 2026 Reviewed by Reviewer name, role 3 sources

Short answer

Day 1 CPT is Curricular Practical Training that an F-1 student is authorized to begin in the first term of a graduate program, instead of after one full academic year. Federal regulations allow this only when the program requires immediate practical training as an integral part of its curriculum. The school's Designated School Official authorizes it, not USCIS.

Key facts

Legal basis
8 CFR 214.2(f)(10)(i) [1]
Who can qualify
Graduate students whose program requires immediate participation in practical training [1]
Who authorizes it
The Designated School Official (DSO), in SEVIS. It is printed on the Form I-20 [3]
USCIS application
None for CPT itself [3]
Effect on OPT
One year or more of full-time CPT ends eligibility for OPT [1]

How Day 1 CPT works

Curricular Practical Training (CPT) is work, an internship or a practicum that is an integral part of an established curriculum. Most F-1 students must be enrolled full time for one academic year before they can use it.

The regulation makes one exception: graduate students whose programs require practical training immediately. "Day 1 CPT" is the informal name for CPT authorized under that exception. It is not a separate visa or a separate status.

How it differs from regular CPT

The rules are the same. Only the start date differs, and only for programs that meet the exception.

What changed in 2026

Schools are applying a stricter test

In August 2026 SEVP sent two broadcast messages to school officials about CPT authorization. Ask the DSO at any school you are considering how its program meets the "integral part of the curriculum" requirement. See rule changes.

Common questions

Is Day 1 CPT a visa?

No. It is a type of work authorization available to some students who already hold F-1 status.

Does USCIS approve Day 1 CPT?

No. The school's DSO authorizes CPT in SEVIS. USCIS may review that history in a later filing.
